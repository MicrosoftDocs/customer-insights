# Scenarios for web tracking and personalization

When you run web tracking, the stream of visitor activity helps you win new customers, convert active visitors, retain existing customers, and support the people who serve them.

Customers adopt the following scenarios first and get the fastest value. Each scenario is a starting point rather than a limit; most organizations begin with one, see it works, and expand from there.

## Acquisition and campaign scenarios

### Attribute web activity to a paid campaign

Tag the destination links in your paid and social campaigns with a campaign parameter, and Customer Insights - Data captures that parameter on every tracked view and action. You can then report on which campaigns drove real engagement on your site, not just clicks, and build segments of the people who responded to a specific campaign.

### Close the loop between an exported segment and the visit it produced

Export a segment to an advertising platform, then recognize the visitors who arrive from that campaign as they land on your site. The round trip from segment to ad to tracked visit to follow-up segment happens without any manual list reconciliation.

### Measure landing page performance by audience

Compare how different segments behave on the same landing page, using the pages they viewed and the elements they selected rather than aggregate page counts. The result tells you which audiences your page actually converts and which ones bounce.

## Conversion scenarios

### Welcome back a returning anonymous visitor

Recognize an unknown visitor who has been to your site before and change what they see on arrival, without asking them to identify themselves. Returning visitors can be shown the category they browsed last time, a continuation of an unfinished task, or an offer that recognizes their repeat interest.

### Recommend based on what the visitor already browsed

Use the specific products, plans, or content a visitor viewed to drive the next thing you show them. Someone who looked at three premium coffee machines sees premium machines, accessories, and a comparison guide rather than the generic homepage merchandising.

### Convert an anonymous visitor into a known customer

When a visitor supplies an email address or phone number, merge their anonymous profile into a known customer profile and keep the entire browsing history that preceded it. New known customers start with a full picture of their interests instead of a blank record.

### Recover an abandoned browse or cart

Detect that a visitor examined a product or started a purchase and then left, and follow up while the intent is still fresh. Because the activity is available within seconds, the follow-up can happen in the same session rather than the next day.

## Personalization scenarios

### Offer an incentive to a high-value segment

Show a discount, upgrade path, or loyalty benefit only to visitors who belong to a segment such as high lifetime value. Define the segment once in Customer Insights - Data and apply it on your website, in journeys, and in exports.

### Suppress offers for customers who already converted

Stop promoting a product to visitors who already own it or already responded to the campaign. Suppression is often the highest-return personalization change an organization makes, because it removes the most visible source of irrelevant messaging.

### Personalize pricing, plan, and renewal pages by tier

Adapt the page a customer sees to the plan, tier, or contract they hold, so pricing and renewal conversations start from what they actually have. This goal is particularly effective on self-service upgrade and renewal paths.

### Test personalization variants without code

Use the Optimizely integration to run your Customer Insights - Data segments as Optimizely audiences and experiment on the experience each audience receives. Marketers can change and measure the experience without a development cycle.

## Segmentation and insight scenarios

### Build intent-based segments from browsing behavior

Create segments from what people did on your site — everyone who viewed a pricing page twice in the last 30 days without requesting a demo, for instance. Behavioral segments capture intent that never appears in transactional data.

### Enrich lead scoring with real-time web signals

Feed web interaction data into your lead scoring model so scores reflect current browsing behavior alongside firmographic and transactional attributes. Sales sees a score that moves when the buyer moves.

### Reunite activity across devices and browsers

A single person visiting from a laptop and a phone starts as two anonymous profiles. Once they sign in on either device, the activity from both consolidates onto one known profile, correcting the double-counting that otherwise distorts engagement measures.

### Surface web activity on the customer timeline

Give sellers and service agents a customer's recent web activity directly on the profile timeline, so the conversation starts from what the customer was just looking at rather than from the last recorded transaction.

## Retention and service scenarios

### Detect churn-risk signals from support and cancellation pages

Treat repeated visits to support, troubleshooting, or cancellation pages as an early churn signal, and act on it while the customer is still on the site. A proactive offer or a routed service contact usually costs less than a win-back campaign.

### Trigger a real-time service follow-up

Use a tracked action — a failed form submission, or repeated visits to the same help article — to trigger a journey or a service task within seconds. The customer is contacted while the problem is still in front of them.

### Prioritize outreach with live buying signals

Rank the accounts and contacts your sellers work by what those people are doing on your site right now, rather than by a nightly refreshed score. Outreach lands during the window when interest is highest.
